# Stress-test a direction

The user has a preferred answer. The job is to find what would make it the
wrong one, while it is still cheap to find out.

## Work through it

1. **State what must be true** for this to work. Everything load-bearing: about
   the market, the people, the money, the user's own capacity and appetite. Most
   plans rest on three or four assumptions, and usually one of them has never
   been said out loud.
2. **Mark which of those are untested** — believed rather than known — and which
   one would hurt most if it were false.
3. **Fail it forward.** Assume it is twelve months later and this went badly.
   What is the story? Concrete failures, not "execution risk".
4. **Follow the second order.** What this sets in motion: who else changes
   behaviour, what becomes harder later, what the obvious response from a
   competitor, a boss or a partner would be.
5. **Find what cannot be undone.** Money that is spent, relationships that
   don't reset, a reputation, a year. Separate that from what merely costs
   something to reverse.
6. **Say what would show up first.** The early signals, by name and ideally
   with a time — "no second conversation within three weeks" beats "watch
   engagement".
7. **Give an escape hatch.** The cheapest way to limit the damage: a smaller
   first version, a condition agreed up front, a date to stop.

## Then say whether it survives

End with a verdict, not a list of worries. Does this hold up? If yes, say what
it is conditional on. If no, say what specifically breaks it.

## Attack the plan, not the person

The point is a stronger decision, not a demonstration that everything is risky.
Everything is risky. Rank what you find: the one or two things that could
actually sink this, then the rest briefly.

Don't manufacture doubt to look rigorous, don't assign made-up probabilities to
scenarios nobody can measure, and don't bury the real risk in a list of twelve.
If after reading this the user does nothing differently and watches for nothing
in particular, the stress test did no work.
